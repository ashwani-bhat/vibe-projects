Train until the loss flattens and the prediction panel looks like the game (~a minute). Then enter the dream: the simulator stops feeding the screen — the net predicts frame t+1 from its own frames t and t−1, plus your paddle input. The opponent you play against in there isn't programmed anymore; it's the net's memory of the opponent. Dreams decay — when the ball dissolves, re-seed.

How it works: a world model in miniature (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018). While the real game runs, transitions (frame t−1, frame t, action) → frame t+1 fill a replay buffer, and an MLP (two 32×32 frames + action one-hot → 384 ReLU → 1024 pixel logits, ~1.2M parameters) trains on per-pixel sigmoid cross-entropy via TensorFlow.js. Dream mode feeds the model its own output back — closed-loop, no simulator — with your action injected each step. The "crisp dream" toggle binarizes the feedback frame, which fights the blur death-spiral that compounding uncertainty causes; turn it off to watch the dream melt. The reality panel runs true physics from the same seed with the same inputs: the divergence you see is exactly the model's error, compounding at 30fps.
