where the errors come from

Measured by counterfactual, not guesswork: each bias is switched off with the same random draws, and the accuracy delta is what it was costing you.

the leaderboard your eval produces

What this is: four systems are ranked by pairwise LLM-judge comparisons over 400 items. Ground truth is known to the simulation and hidden from the judges. Each judge carries documented, reproducible biases — position (prefers whatever came first), verbosity (prefers the longer answer), self-preference (prefers its own model family), plus noise. The candidates are built so verbosity is uncorrelated with quality: the best model is terse and the second-best is the wordiest. That's the trap. A length-loving judge scores ~70% agreement — a number most teams would ship — while ranking the second-best model first. Aggregate accuracy hides it; the leaderboard doesn't. The lab shows the fixes that actually work (swap the order, cap length credit, recuse the family, diversify the panel) and what each one costs, because a council of four with swapping is eight judge calls per item.
